# The v2.7.0 BeeAdHoc anchor

**Status: complete (2026-11-01). The anchor MISSES**: BeeAdHoc's control
overhead is below AODV's and DSDV's at 3 of the 5 pause times, and the
pre-registered gate asked for 4. Under the comparator rule (AGENTS.md,
Conventions) the `beeadhoc` arm ships as a written infeasibility verdict: its
family numbers do not count. The verdict is recorded on
[#800](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/800). Re-reading it
against the GECCO 2005 paper, once in hand, is
[#815](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/815).

## What was fixed before dispatch

The plan file
[`tools/bench/campaigns/v2.7.0-beeadhoc-anchor.yaml`](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/blob/main/tools/bench/campaigns/v2.7.0-beeadhoc-anchor.yaml)
(generation `v270a`) holds the source, the cell and the rule; this section
summarises it.

- **Source.** The PG 460 final report (Univ. Dortmund, 2006, hdl 2003/22405),
  ch. 7, the one the arm was written from
  ([fidelity sheet](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/blob/main/ns3/baselines/adaptive/README.md#beeadhoc--fidelity-sheet)).
  Its numbers are in figures only, so the anchor is held to the trends its
  text states: BeeAdHoc "sends the smallest number of control packet[s]",
  constant over pause time (§7.2.5); lowest energy per kB (§7.2.2); best
  delay (§7.2.3) and throughput (§7.2.4).
- **Cell.** `routing-compare --scenario=paper` with every knob explicit: 100
  nodes, 3000 × 1000 m, 900 s, 20 CBR flows at 4 packets/s, random waypoint
  at U[0, 10] m/s, pause 1, 30, 100, 500, 1000 s, two-ray, 250 m range,
  UDP, image 3.42-opt, seeds 1–10 (two blocks of five).
- **Deviations.** 64-byte data packets instead of 512 (the packet rate is
  kept, so the offered load is 1/8 of the source's); no DSR arm, so AODV and
  DSDV are the comparators; 10 seeds instead of 5.
- **Two settings.** The arm's defaults (`control`), and no expanding ring
  (`ringoff`, `InitialScoutTtl=10`), because #806 found that the ring's
  starting TTL, which the report does not give, decides the arm's delay. The
  `ringoff` jobs run BeeAdHoc alone and pair with the `control` cell's AODV
  and DSDV rows on the same seeds.
- **Rule.** PRIMARY, the gate: at each pause time, the paired 95 % CI of
  (BeeAdHoc − other) NRL lies wholly below zero against AODV and against
  DSDV; the anchor passes at ≥ 4 of 5 pause times in the default setting.
  SECONDARY, reported and not gated: energy per delivered packet, mean delay,
  PDR, throughput, the same comparisons for `ringoff`, and the in-packet
  route bytes (`##INPKT##`, #808).

This is the second dispatch. The first one, before
[#831](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/pull/831), was void: at
4 packets/s the arm made the application re-send packets, so its cells
counted
duplicate deliveries. The cells here were run on the fixed code (`f14d5cc`),
and `scenario_check results` passes every one with its duplicate-delivery
rule (0 FAIL; the only WARNs are the path-diversity "churn-dominated" note,
which concerns AntHocNet rows this cell does not have).

## Results

Cells: `results/cells/anchor-bee-p<pause>-v270a.txt` (all three protocols)
and `anchor-bee-p<pause>-v270a-ringoff.txt` (BeeAdHoc, ring off). Every
interval is a paired 95 % CI over 10 seeds (t = 2.262); \* marks one that
excludes zero.

**3 of 5: the anchor misses.** What reproduces is the shape of §7.2.5:
BeeAdHoc's control load is flat over pause time (9.4–10.5) and far below
AODV's everywhere. What does not is the ranking against DSDV: DSDV's
triggered updates fall away as the nodes stop moving, and from pause 500 s
on it costs the same as BeeAdHoc. Counted in bytes (`nrl_bytes`) the arm is
*above* DSDV at 500 and 1000 s (+0.87 [+0.20, +1.54]\*, +1.48 [+0.50,
+2.47]\*), and the route it carries in every data packet (`##INPKT##`) adds
another 1.1–1.6 to its byte NRL (`nrlBytesIncl` 8.6–9.6 against `nrl_bytes`
7.6–8.1). The report says its own DSDV runs were "left out in some cases due
to errors", so the source's DSDV comparison may be thinner than its sentence;
that is for #815 to read against GECCO, not a reason to move the gate.

Throughput follows PDR (the same flows at a fixed rate). BeeAdHoc's mean
delay is 196–295 ms against AODV's 57–76 and DSDV's 17–43.

- **Energy (§7.2.2) reproduces**: lowest per delivered packet at every pause
  time, tied with DSDV at 1000 s.
- **Delivery reproduces** where the report implies it: BeeAdHoc delivers the
  most packets until the network is nearly static, where DSDV catches up.
- **Delay (§7.2.3) is contradicted** in the default setting, by 120–280 ms.
  Almost all of it is the source's hold queue while the expanding ring
  searches (#805, #806).

| 1000 | 12.87 | +3.56\* | +3.44\* | 23.2 | −37.1\* | +6.6\* |

Without the ring the arm trades exactly as #806 found on the grid cell: mean
delay falls by 146–272 ms to below AODV's at every pause time (still 6–11 ms
above DSDV's), for about +4 control packets per delivered packet, which puts
its NRL above DSDV's everywhere. Against AODV the NRL stays below at every
pause time. PDR and energy per packet move by under 1 pp and 0.02 J. So
neither setting gives the report's "smallest control load *and* best delay"
together: the ring buys the control load with the delay, and dropping it buys
the delay back with the control load.

## What follows

- `beeadhoc` stays in the module and in the per-PR delivery smoke; it is not
  run in the family comparison, and no page quotes its family numbers.
- [#815](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/815) re-reads the
  setup and the claims against GECCO 2005 when the paper is in hand. A
  different source setup is a new pre-registered anchor, not a re-reading of
  this one.
