# The v2.2.0 re-baseline campaign — plan

**Status: complete (2026-10-16). No default moves; the control generation is
v2.2.0's corpus.** The [results](#results) are at the end of this page. Above
them, unchanged, is the pre-registration for [#565](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/565).
It was written and merged before the first run, so the order of operations, the
arms and the decision rule were fixed before any number existed. The same rule
governed the [v1.5.0 campaign](v1.5.0-campaign.md).

[← Benchmark index](../benchmarks.md) · [Methodology](methodology.md) · [Roadmap](../roadmap.md)

## What this campaign decides

v2.2.0 changes, or may change, protocol behaviour in three places. Each was
measured as a weak spot by v1.9.0 or v2.0.0:

| change | issue | the question | arm(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RepairHoldCap` 0 → 200 ms | [#433](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/433) | The v1.5.0 flip moved only `ReconvHoldCap`. The ablation that chose 200 ms moved both caps and recovered about 168 ms more tail. Is the second cap worth its delivery cost on its own? | `repair200` |
| the thesis's SINR link metric | [#181](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/181) | The 2007 thesis reports SINR as its best metric and used it for every headline result. Does it beat `classic` here? | `sinr` |
| VANET reconvergence | [#537](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/537) | 36 % of VANET traffic is lost to reconvergence, because corners break links about every 0.5 s. Do the two knobs #537 names close the gap? | `hello500`, `reconv1s` (VANET screen first) |

Measured one at a time, each change would re-measure every family page. That
is the [#365](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/365) failure. So
this is **one** campaign: one control generation, every arm against it on
identical seeds, and one restatement of every page at the end.

## Arms

All arms run at one commit: the merge commit of the last code PR in phase 0,
recorded as `##PROV##` in every cell.

| arm | AntHocNet attributes | protocols | cells |
|---|---|---|---|
| `control` | shipped defaults | every arm the cell publishes (AntHocNet, the baselines, the oracle) | all 18 |
| `repair200` | `RepairHoldCap=200ms` | `anthocnet` | all 18 |
| `sinr` | `LinkMetric=sinr` | `anthocnet` | all 18 |
| `hello500` | `HelloInterval=500ms` | `anthocnet` | VANET `main` first (screen) |
| `reconv1s` | `ReconvHoldCap=1s` | `anthocnet` | VANET `main` first (screen) |

Notes:

- **Only `control` runs the baselines.** AODV, OLSR, DSDV, the oracle, AOMDV and
  GPSR do not read any AntHocNet attribute, so running them in every arm would
  spend runtime re-measuring byte-identical numbers.
- **Attribution control:** one cell per family also runs `repair200` with every
  protocol. Its baseline rows must be byte-identical to `control`'s, or the
  harness moved and no delta in that family is attributable. This is
  `sweep_summary.py --vs`'s exit-1 rule, applied by hand to `##BENCH##` cells.
- **The control arm is also the re-baseline.** If no default moves, the
  `control` cells are published as v2.2.0's corpus. The GPSR fix in #658 changes
  the GPSR rows of the six-arm cells, and the control generation records that.
- **On the satellite cells, `sinr` reads "no sample"** on every point-to-point
  ISL, because those links have no PHY SINR (ADR-0024). There it is the
  hop-count metric, not SINR. It is still measured, because ADR-0019 requires
  every family, and the satellite page states what the arm is there.

## Pre-registered decision rule

Every comparison is paired per seed against `control`, on the same 20 seeds:
- PDR, NRL: t-interval;
- delay99: bootstrap;
- Wilcoxon signed-rank p;
- computed with `bench_parse.py --ab` (`##BENCH##` cells) or the leo/isl
  summaries.

Per arm and cell, the verdict is `PAIRED-IMPROVED`, `PAIRED-WORSE` or `NOISE`
under the script's rule.

**A default moves only if, across all 18 cells:**
1. no cell is `PAIRED-WORSE` on PDR (paired 95 % CI entirely below zero), and
2. no cell is `PAIRED-WORSE` on delay99 by more than 10 % while PDR is
   `NOISE`, and
3. at least one family is `PAIRED-IMPROVED` on PDR or on delay99.

This is [ADR-0019](../adr/0019-network-families-change-the-evaluation-not-the-protocol.md):
a change is earned everywhere or it is not the default.

**Otherwise the change ships selectable and off:**
- `repair200` keeps its attribute;
- `sinr` keeps `LinkMetric=sinr`;
- the per-family deltas go into the mechanism × regime table in
  [network-regimes.md](../network-regimes.md), so a reader who cares about one
  family can still use it.

**#537's two knobs are screened on VANET `main` first.**
- An arm that is not `PAIRED-IMPROVED` on PDR there is recorded on #537 and
  stops.
- An arm that is goes on to the other 17 cells and faces the rule above.
- If neither closes most of the reconvergence loss, #537's finding stands. Its
  measured cause is link lifetime, which the preflight now reports, so v2.3.0's
  link-lifetime mechanism (#574) keeps its place, per the roadmap's trigger.

**If two defaults move,** their combination is a different protocol from
either arm. A final `combined` arm then runs in all 18 cells with every
protocol, and **that** generation is what the pages publish.

## Phase order

### Phase 0: code, no runs

1. The SINR metric and its attribute (#181/#144, ADR-0024), merged with its
   own tests and the WASM parity gate.
2. #537's preflight rule. `scenario_check.py preflight` now WARNs that a link
   round a street corner lives about `streetWidth / speed` (1.0 s on the vanet
   preset) rather than the 10.6 s distance bound. Merged with this plan.
3. Every arm's attribute exists and is printed in `##CONFIG## attr`, so each
   cell records which arm it is.
4. `scenario_check.py preflight` passes (or only WARNs, with the WARNs stated)
   for every cell's inputs.

### Phase 1: control

All 18 cells, every protocol, 20 seeds, at the phase-0 commit. Each cell is
validated with `scenario_check.py results` and its anchor floor where it has
one. A FAIL stops the campaign: it is a harness regression, and nothing
measured on top of it is comparable.

### Phase 2: arms

`repair200` and `sinr` in all 18 cells, plus the attribution control. The VANET
screen arms run alongside, on `main`. Each verdict is recorded on its issue
when its last cell lands (ADR-0013).

### Phase 3: decide and restate

Apply the rule. Then:
- run the `combined` arm if two defaults moved;
- restate every family page once, at the final configuration, with provenance:
  [grid.md](grid.md), [static-mesh.md](static-mesh.md), the FANET and VANET
  scenario pages, and the two satellite pages;
- update the roadmap's v2.2.0 row.

## What would make this plan wrong

- **A phase-1 cell disagrees with the published corpus on a baseline row, and
  no harness change explains it.** Stop and investigate first: the published
  baselines were measured on the same images.
- **An arm's attribute does not reach the run** (its `##CONFIG## attr` line
  shows the default). The cell is void; re-dispatch it.
- **`sinr` behaves identically to `control` on the Wi-Fi cells.** That would
  mean the PHY hook is not delivering samples. Diagnose with a one-cell
  `runs=2` dispatch before spending 20 seeds anywhere else.

**Verdict: no AntHocNet default changes in v2.2.0.** All four arms fail rule 1:
each has at least one cell where PDR is `PAIRED-WORSE`, with the paired 95 % CI
entirely below zero. Each ships as it already did, selectable and off. No two
defaults moved, so the `combined` arm did not run, and the `control` generation
is v2.2.0's corpus.

### Validity

- **Phase 1 (control).**
  - All 18 cells, every protocol, 20 seeds, at `a8a16991`.
  - Every Wi-Fi cell's rows match the published corpus.
  - The satellite AODV rows differ only through stock AODV's pointer-keyed socket map ([#362](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/362)). On a multi-interface node that map makes AODV's iteration order depend on heap layout. The paired deltas are within noise.
  - `scenario_check.py results` gives 0 FAIL, except the known [#431](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/431) oracle-hop findings on the three Nakagami cells. They are identical in kind and size to the published cells'.
- **Phase 2 (arms).**
  - The arms ran at `main`, because creating a tag was not available to the campaign. That was `ee1b0ee8` for the first dispatches, then `da146c66`, `65b7bde5` and `8da0fe27`.
  - Between those commits the only change under `core/`, `ns3/` or the benchmark workflows is one comment in `ns3/doctor.sh`.
  - The phase-0 → `ee1b0ee8` change is the clang-format reformat ([#35](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/35)) plus the replay inputs ([#639](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/639)). Two checks show the reformat changed no behaviour:
    - **Recheck:** grid rwp/tworay at `main`, defaults, every protocol. Every `##` row is byte-identical to its phase-1 cell.
    - **Attribution control:** `repair200` with every protocol in grid rwp/tworay, static mesh, FANET sparse, VANET sparse ×2 and satellite base. Every baseline row is byte-identical to `control`'s, with two expected exceptions:
      - `##COMMON##`, the set AntHocNet and the baseline both delivered, which moves with AnthocNet by construction;
      - satellite AODV, as above.
- **Every arm reached its run:** each cell's `##CONFIG## attr` line shows the arm's value.
- **Pairing:** every comparison is paired per seed against `control`, through `bench_parse.py --ab --proto anthocnet`.
  - Most cells have n = 20.
  - VANET main ran as five blocks of 4 seeds, and VANET sparse as two blocks of 10, so each block is paired on its own.
  - On n = 4, the Wilcoxon p floor is 0.125, so the blocks are read through their t-intervals and their sign consistency.

Holding packets for up to 1 s across a reconvergence pays where a break is clean and a new route arrives within the hold:
- VANET main +5.3 pp, VANET sparse +6.2 pp, FANET sparse +4.4 pp;
- the price is delay99, +180–600 ms.

On a fading channel, many "breaks" are transient fades. Held packets queue behind ones that would have got through, so PDR falls (by up to 6 pp) and delay99 rises by 300–470 ms. On the LEO shells the hold adds about 100–120 ms of delay99 for at most +0.8 pp.

### What this means

- **The defaults stand as shipped.** This is ADR-0019 working as intended: every arm wins somewhere, and no arm wins everywhere.
- **The per-regime recommendations** are in [network-regimes.md §7](../network-regimes.md#7-measured-the-v220-knobs-per-regime). The VANET page has a [tuning section](scenarios/vanet.md#tuning-for-urban-vanet-v220-campaign) for users who run that regime.
- **#537's finding stands.**
  - The three knobs each claw back part of VANET main's reconvergence loss: `reconv1s` +5.3 pp, `hello500` +1.9 pp, `repair200` +1.1 pp.
  - None closes the 39.6 pp gap to the oracle.
  - The measured cause, link lifetime at corners, keeps v2.3.0's link-lifetime mechanism ([#574](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/574)) in place.
- **#181 is answered.** The thesis's best metric is not the best default on ns-3's PHY and MAC across families. `LinkMetric=classic` (delay + hops) stays the default.
