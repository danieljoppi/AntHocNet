## What this campaign decides

**No default.** It has a single arm, `control`, at the shipped defaults:
v2.3.0's, with quiet mode on. What it changes is what AntHocNet is compared
against. [ADR-0026](../adr/0026-baselines-are-family-native.md) makes
baselines family-native: each family is compared against the protocols its
own literature uses, plus its oracle. This campaign re-measures the five
published families on those sets. Its cells become the v2.4.0 generation every
family page quotes, and the input to the cross-family view
([#711](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/711)).

It is also where the four new comparators get their 20-seed cells:

- HWMP ([#708](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/708));
- GPCR ([#709](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/709));
- 3-D GPSR ([#710](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/710));
- OPSPF ([#707](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/707)).

Their PRs merged them with an anchor and the delivery smoke, on short runs
only.

## The static-mesh cell

The field, load and seeds are v2.3.0's. Two things change, for every arm in
the cell:

- **802.11a at a fixed 6 Mbit/s** (`--standard=80211a
  --rateManager=constant6`; maintainer decision 2026-10-19, #708). ns-3's
  802.11s tunes every mesh interface to 5 GHz channel 100, so HWMP cannot run
  on the corpus's 802.11b radio. The disk channel ignores frequency, so the
  topology is v2.3.0's. The rate is not: these rows are not paired with the
  802.11b record.
- **`--ns3::dot11s::PeerLink::MaxBeaconLoss=20`** (maintainer decision
  2026-10-20). The [finding on #708](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/708#issuecomment-6294616370)
  has the evidence. At ns-3's default of 2, a peer link closes after two
  missed beacons. On this 50-node field the path-selection floods lose
  beacons, links churn, each closure floods more path selection, and HWMP
  delivers 56.5 % over 900 s (seed 1). With the parameter at 20 the churn
  stops (peering frames 203 k → 1.7 k) and HWMP delivers 88.9 %. IEEE
  802.11s does not fix this value; it is ns-3's choice. The attribute
  reaches only `dot11s` peer links, so it is inert for the other arms.

The HWMP gates (#716) run 16 nodes for at most 120 s. They prove the arm
routes, not how it scales; this cell is where the scaling shows.

## Seed blocks

No job may run past the 6 h runner limit:

- **The grid cells** run as two 10-seed blocks. v2.3.0's Gauss-Markov/Nakagami
  control took 4 h 43 min for five protocols, and AOMDV is a sixth.
- **The static mesh** runs as four 5-seed blocks. One HWMP seed costs about
  15 CPU-minutes locally, four times AntHocNet's.
- **VANET** keeps v2.3.0's blocks: main 5 × 4 seeds, sparse 2 × 10.
- **Everything else** runs 20 seeds in one job. One S1 storm seed runs OPSPF
  and the oracle together in under 6 min (#721).

`campaign.py collect` concatenates the blocks into one file per cell, and
`bench_parse.py` pairs them by seed. The plan is 44 jobs.

## How the results are read

Each reading is fixed now, before any number exists.

1. **The family gap to the oracle.** AntHocNet minus the oracle, PDR in pp,
   with a paired 95 % CI per cell. This is the family-neutral yardstick
   (#711).
2. **AntHocNet against the best native baseline.** The best native baseline
   is the one with the highest mean PDR over the 20 seeds, with ties broken
   by lower delay99. It is chosen per cell from the data, by this rule only.
   The comparison is paired per seed (`bench_parse.py --ab` style, PDR,
   delay99 and NRL), with the verdict vocabulary of the earlier campaigns.
3. **The comparators' own acceptance criteria:**
   - HWMP, the #708 criteria;
   - GPCR above GPSR on the VANET cells, which is what #709 measures;
   - gpsr3d on the two FANET cells (#710);
   - OPSPF on the three shells, with AntHocNet − OPSPF as the satellite
     headline (#707).
   Each issue gets its verdict, with the numbers, when its cells land
   (ADR-0013).
4. **The OPSPF / oracle reading.** OPSPF beats the 1 s hop oracle on failure
   cells by design. The oracle there is not a delivery bound
   ([#720](https://github.com/danieljoppi/AntHocNet/issues/720)). The pages
   say so wherever both appear, and no AntHocNet gap on the LEO failure cells
   is called a gap to "the bound".

## Validity checks, run before any reading

- **Attribution.** AntHocNet's core and model code have not changed since
  v2.3.0. `bench_parse.py --control` against v2.3.0's published cells should
  therefore show these rows identical, seed for seed:
  - every AntHocNet row, everywhere except the static mesh;
  - every kept baseline row (AODV, OLSR, DSDV, oracle; geo-greedy and the
    two oracles on LEO).
  Two exceptions are expected:
  - stock AODV on multi-interface satellite nodes may read ORDER-ONLY (#675);
  - **GPSR on VANET will differ**: the #717 fix (#718) moved GPSR's first
    hello after stream pinning, which changes its draws. That is the reason
    for the difference, recorded on #717.
  Any other difference means the harness moved. That family's readings wait
  until it is explained.
- **The static mesh has no attribution control:** every arm's radio changed.
  Its consistency check is the oracle's mean hop count (the `##ROUTE##`
  line). It depends on the topology, not on the rate, so it must stay within
  1 % of v2.3.0's on every seed. Seed 1 reads 2.9242 against 2.9241, with
  PDR 99.3 % on both.
- **Every arm reached its run.** On the Wi-Fi cells, the `##CONFIG##` line
  shows the protocols and the static-mesh radio. On the static mesh, the
  `# hwmp` diag line shows peering frames in the low thousands, not hundreds
  of thousands. If it does not, the `MaxBeaconLoss` attribute did not reach
  the run, and the cell is void.
- **`scenario_check.py results`** on every cell. OPSPF's rule (#721) FAILs a
  run that floods in a failure-free cell or never floods in a failure cell.
  The #431 oracle-hop findings on the Nakagami cells are known.
- **Preflight.** `scenario_check.py preflight` on the cells this plan
  changes:
  - static mesh: WARN only, the 802.11a and HWMP notes;
  - FANET with gpsr3d: WARN only;
  - VANET main with GPCR: WARN only;
  - grid with AOMDV: WARN only.
  vanet-sparse FAILs the degree rule, as it has since its definition: it is
  the deliberately partitioned street cell. Its protocol set does not change
  that.

## What would make this plan wrong

- **A kept baseline row moves** beyond the two expected exceptions. Stop: the
  harness moved.
- **OPSPF floods zero LSAs on walker16, or floods on a cell without
  failures.** The arm is mis-wired; the results rule catches both.
- **HWMP's peering frames climb** into the hundreds of thousands on any seed.
  The attribute did not take, or the churn has a second cause; diagnose on
  #708 before publishing.
- **A comparator falls far below its short-run numbers** on the 20-seed cell.
  Two examples: gpsr3d under 80 % on fanet-main (it read 89.2 % on 2 seeds),
  or GPCR under GPSR on vanet-main. Check the cell before reading it as a
  result.

## Phase order

1. **Code, no runs.** Done: #716 (HWMP), #718 (GPCR), #719 (gpsr3d), #721
   (OPSPF). Merge this plan.
2. **Campaign.** All 44 jobs at `main`, at most 12 runs active.
3. **Collect and check** (the validity checks above), then post each
   comparator's verdict on its issue.
4. **Restate.** Each family page publishes its v2.4.0 native set, with
   migrant rows marked retired. #711 lands with the new cross-family view.
   The roadmap's v2.4.0 row records the comparators as done.

## Results

**Every validity check passed as pre-registered, and every family page now
reads its native set.** The 44 jobs ran at `main` (`f0aedd3`, the plan's
merge) from 2026-10-20 16:03 UTC to 23:37 UTC, at most
12 active. None
failed. The cells are `results/cells/<cell>-v240.txt`, one per cell, and
`leo-<shell>-<protocol>-v240.txt` on the LEO shells.

### Validity

- **Attribution against v2.3.0.** Every AntHocNet row and every kept
  baseline row is byte-identical to v2.3.0's published cells, seed for seed,
  in all 30 cells. That covers AODV, OLSR, DSDV and the oracle on the MANET
  grid and FANET, AODV and the oracle on VANET, the oracle on the ISL grid,
  and geo-greedy and both oracles on the LEO shells. Exactly the two
  pre-registered exceptions differ:
  - **GPSR on VANET:** 0 of 20 seeds identical, from the #717 fix (#718).
  - **The static mesh, every arm:** the 802.11a radio.
- **The static-mesh topology check.** The oracle's mean hop count stays
  within 1 % of v2.3.0's on every seed (the largest difference is 0.99 %).
- **Every arm reached its run.** The `##CONFIG##` lines show each family's
  protocol set and the static mesh's radio. HWMP's peering frames read
  1575–2413 per seed in the first block, so `MaxBeaconLoss=20` took.
- **`scenario_check.py results`.** 0 FAIL on the static mesh, FANET, VANET,
  ISL-grid and LEO cells. On the MANET grid, two Nakagami cells keep 2 and 3
  FAILs of the known #431 oracle-hop class; v2.3.0's same cells had 8 and 15,
  and ssrwp/Nakagami goes from 12 to 0. The drop comes from the cell, not
  from the checker (`main`'s checker gives the same counts). The
  identity-matched hop rule compares arms on the packets that every arm in
  the run delivered. AOMDV, new here and the lowest deliverer, shrinks that
  common set: on Gauss-Markov/Nakagami seed 4 it falls from 10 364 to 8 702
  packets. Fewer of the fading-path packets that trip the rule remain.
- **A checker fix this campaign needed.** `scenario_check.py` put the
  summary tables of a cell collected from seed blocks into one group. It then
  compared block 1's oracle against the other blocks' arms, and reported 3
  false FAILs on the static mesh. It now groups each block by its
  `firstRun`, with a test case.

What the table says, family by family:

- **MANET grid.** AntHocNet delivers 5.8–11.3 pp more than the best native
  baseline in all six cells, and on two-ray it delivers more than the
  link-blind oracle. OLSR keeps a far shorter tail on two-ray (about 21 ms
  delay99 against AntHocNet's 171–218 ms) and less overhead everywhere.
  AOMDV, new to the grid, is last in every cell (55–72 %).
- **Static mesh.** OLSR leads: 99.98 % against AntHocNet's 99.34 %, at NRL
  1.78 against 4.17. HWMP is third at 91.0 %. AntHocNet − HWMP is +8.34 pp,
  delay99 −1097 ms, NRL −45.3, all paired-significant.
- **FANET.** AntHocNet leads both cells. On FANET main the best native
  baseline is 3-D GPSR (88.3 %), +2.16 pp over AODV. On the sparse cell AODV
  leads the natives, and 3-D GPSR trails it by 7.9 pp: voids are geographic
  routing's weakness.
- **VANET.** AntHocNet ties AODV on delivery in both cells (−0.16 and
  +0.15 pp, both CIs straddle 0), with a tail 1.9–7.9 s shorter at
  13–20 more NRL. Both geographic arms trail AODV by 7–16 pp. The gap to the
  oracle on VANET main (39.6 pp) is still the largest of any cell.
- **ISL grid.** AntHocNet equals the oracle's delivery on all four cells.
  No native baseline is measured here yet (OPSPF on `isl-grid` is deferred).
- **LEO shells.** OPSPF beats AntHocNet by 6.2 pp on walker16 and by 16.9–17.5
  pp on Starlink S1. That is **the headline satellite comparison #707 asked
  for, and it goes against AntHocNet.** OPSPF buys it with 5–20× the control:
  NRL 499 against 98 on walker16, and 16 357–19 399 against 934–974 on S1.
  Its floods are also only accounted, not transmitted (three simplifications,
  all disclosed on [leo-walker.md](satellite/leo-walker.md)). OPSPF also sits
  at or above the 1 s hop oracle (+0.55, +1.54 and +1.04 pp), as #720
  predicted.
